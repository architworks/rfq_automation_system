# Design Decisions and Tradeoffs

This page explains the decisions that shaped TenderLens and the tradeoffs they introduce.

## 1. Framework first, extraction second

### Decision

Generate and lock the evaluation framework before the vendor documents are evaluated.

### Why

This is the strongest decision in the system. It keeps:

- rubric generation
- vendor question generation
- extraction
- normalization
- scoring
- recommendation

aligned to one shared framework object.

### Tradeoff

Framework generation can take time. That is acceptable because the time is spent building a stronger and less subjective downstream evaluation basis.

## 2. AI where judgment is needed, deterministic logic where governance is needed

### Decision

Use AI for ambiguous or unstructured interpretation, but keep deterministic logic for normalization, gating, and official award math.

### Why

This gives the system flexibility where messy documents demand it, but keeps procurement governance stable where the logic should not drift.

### Tradeoff

The architecture is more complex than “ask one model to do everything,” but it is far more explainable and controllable.

## 3. Official result separated from advisory insight

### Decision

Keep QCBS 70/30 as the official result and show LCS, QBS, and contextual AI scenarios as advisory only.

### Why

This preserves a strong distinction between:

- the governed procurement basis
- analytical exploration of alternate lenses

### Tradeoff

The UI and documentation must work harder to communicate that distinction clearly.

## 4. Commercial pricing uses one canonical extraction path

### Decision

Structured commercial pricing is treated as canonical in the requested schedule answers rather than spread across multiple extraction buckets.

### Why

Pricing is inherently tabular and needs one stable source for normalization and comparability.

### Tradeoff

The extraction prompt and schema have to be stricter, but downstream commercial logic becomes cleaner and more reliable.

## 5. Explainability is a first-class output

### Decision

Carry evidence anchors, normalized fields, and buyer-readable reasoning throughout the pipeline.

### Why

A procurement system cannot stop at “Vendor A scored 82.” It must also show:

- what was extracted
- how it was normalized
- why a criterion passed or failed
- why the official winner won

### Tradeoff

This increases model and UI complexity, but it is necessary for auditability and buyer trust.

## 6. Prototype constraints were accepted deliberately

### Decision

Keep the current implementation lightweight:

- no auth
- no persistent database
- one document per vendor
- in-memory session state

### Why

The assignment is about proving the end-to-end system design, not fully productionizing it.

### Tradeoff

Operational robustness is intentionally limited. If this moved toward production, the next major investments would be:

- durable storage
- job orchestration
- collaboration
- operational monitoring
- stronger ingestion coverage

## What I would improve next

- durable background execution for slow AI stages
- persistent storage and audit history
- more robust Word, PowerPoint, and spreadsheet fidelity handling
- better seeded benchmark datasets for repeated evaluation testing
- stronger reviewer workflows around framework approval and award sign-off

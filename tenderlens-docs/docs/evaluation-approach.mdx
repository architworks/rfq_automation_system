# Evaluation Approach

TenderLens is designed to evaluate vendors in a way that is both **strong enough for procurement logic** and **explainable enough for review**. The prototype does not collapse everything into one vague “AI score.” It separates:

- qualification
- technical quality
- commercial comparison
- official recommendation
- advisory AI insights

## Criterion taxonomy

The rubric uses four criterion types:

### 1. MAC
Mandatory acceptance criteria are hard gates. If a vendor fails a MAC, they should not progress as an award-eligible vendor.

Typical examples:

- legal or regulatory capability
- mandatory compliance support
- other pass/fail conditions the buyer explicitly requires

### 2. Technical scored-only
These criteria contribute to the technical score, but they do not have their own individual cutoff.

Typical examples:

- overall campaign solution quality
- strength of the integrated delivery model
- quality of strategic thinking

### 3. Technical cutoff-backed
These criteria contribute to the technical score and also enforce a minimum acceptable quality floor for that criterion.

Typical examples:

- governance quality
- risk management quality
- areas where a weak answer is too risky to ignore even if other technical areas are strong

### 4. Commercial
These criteria are used for pricing completeness, comparability, and final commercial ranking. They are kept outside the technical qualification gate.

## Technical gate

The technical gate is layered. A vendor must:

1. pass all MAC checks
2. pass any critical technical cutoffs
3. meet the aggregate technical threshold

Only vendors that clear the technical gate become eligible for formal commercial comparison and official award ranking.

This is important because the system should not reward the cheapest price if the vendor is technically weak or fails mandatory requirements.

## Commercial evaluation

Commercial evaluation starts only after the technically qualified pool is known.

The system then:

- checks line-item pricing completeness
- verifies comparability of quoted values
- normalizes supported currencies into the RFQ currency
- converts only supported weight and volume units mathematically
- computes comparable totals

The official commercial score is based on the standard formula:

`100 * lowest_comparable_total / vendor_total`

That means the lowest comparable total gets a commercial score of 100, and other vendors scale downward from there.

## Official result versus advisory AI insight

TenderLens deliberately separates:

- **Official result**: the governed QCBS 70/30 award outcome
- **AI insights**: advisory views such as `LCS`, `QBS`, and contextual AI-generated scenarios

The advisory layer is useful, but it does not override the official procurement basis.

## Why this reduces subjectivity

The key to reducing subjectivity is not “remove AI completely.” The key is to use AI in the right place and keep deterministic logic where the system needs a stable, auditable rule.

TenderLens does that by:

- using AI to propose the framework and handle messy-document interpretation
- using deterministic rules for official normalization and commercial math
- applying a fixed official award basis after qualification is established

## Where AI is used

:::tip Where AI is used
- Proposing the rubric structure, question set, schedules, and threshold rationale
- Extracting answers, claims, pricing fields, and evidence anchors from vendor files
- Scoring narrative technical criteria where a qualitative judgment is still needed
- Producing advisory scenario explanations and buyer-facing reasoning summaries
:::

## Where AI is not used

:::info Where AI is not used
- MAC enforcement once the framework is locked
- Technical threshold and cutoff enforcement once the scores exist
- Currency conversion and supported unit conversion
- Commercial score formula
- Official QCBS 70/30 arithmetic
- Final separation between official result and advisory outputs
:::

## What the buyer gets at the end

By the end of the workflow, the buyer does not just get “a winner.” The buyer gets:

- exact disqualification reasons
- vendor-level technical and commercial traceability
- normalized pricing visibility
- evidence-backed evaluation summaries
- one official result and clearly separated advisory alternatives
